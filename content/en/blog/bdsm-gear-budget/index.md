## Complete kit or individual pieces {#kit-or-pieces}

Kits run from 39.99 € to 157.00 €, with ten references available. The trade-off is subtler than it looks.

The kit wins on three counts: lower price per piece, guaranteed visual coherence, and clean gift packaging. It loses on a single but heavy point: it imposes sensation choices that nobody can make on the user's behalf. An eight-piece kit almost always contains two or three accessories that will never be used, because the sensation does not match.

The rule that works: kit while preferences are unknown, individual pieces as soon as they take shape. In practice, a first kit at 57.50 €, then pieces chosen one by one. Going straight to the 157 € kit without knowing what appeals means paying a lot for a learning curve that runs perfectly well at 47 €.

## What costs money and serves no purpose {#wasted-spend}

Three items come up regularly in regretted baskets.

The first is the accessory bought twice through unfamiliar vocabulary. Flogger, whip and cat o' nine tails often describe the same object depending on the shop, and many people end up with two near-identical pieces. The [best BDSM flogger](/en/blog/best-bdsm-flogger/) comparison sets out those naming differences.

The second is heavy restraint hardware bought too early. Bars, hogties and furniture need daily practice to pay for themselves, and often turn out to be unusable in an ordinary flat.

The third is quieter: accessories that cannot be cleaned. Untreated leather and natural-fibre rope take neither water nor disinfectant, which limits their use to a single partner. That is not a flaw, provided it is known before purchase rather than after.

### Is it better to buy a BDSM kit or individual accessories?

The kit while preferences are unknown, individual pieces as soon as they take shape. The kit costs less per piece and guarantees a coherent set, but it imposes sensation choices nobody can make on the user's behalf, and it almost always contains two or three accessories that will go unused. It depends heavily on the category. The cheapest are masks and blindfolds from 6.90 € and nipple clamps from 7.50 €. No, and that is the costliest mistake on this kind of purchase. Two or three accessories are enough for the first sessions, and the experience gained with them determines what is worth buying next. A complete set bought before knowing one's own thresholds statistically ends up half unused. The sequence that works is this: visual deprivation and soft restraint first, light impact next, pressure stimulation after that, and heavy restraint hardware last, if it is still justified.
