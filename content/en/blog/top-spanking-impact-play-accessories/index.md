## 1. The paddle, the forgiving entry point {#paddle}

A paddle is a rigid or flexible blade, in leather or faux leather, that strikes flat. Its broad surface spreads the force over several dozen square centimetres, which produces a thuddy, warm impact that is very easy to read for the person receiving it.

That surface is exactly what makes it the best first accessory. A misplaced paddle stroke stays a misplaced stroke with no consequence, because the force is diluted. The same deviation with a cane leaves a clean mark for several days. For a first session, margin for error matters more than finesse of sensation.

At **1969**, the family runs to ten models from 12.90 to 44 euros, from the in-house Tender paddle to full leather pieces in the Rouge line, including a padded ping-pong paddle at 44 euros whose foam absorbs part of the blow. What separates a good paddle from a bad one comes down to one word: the edge. A raised seam or a sharp rim concentrates all the force along a line and turns a diffuse impact into a cut. ## 2. The flogger, the widest range of sensation {#flogger}

A flogger is a bundle of falls fixed to a handle. Contrary to a stubborn assumption, it is not a violent accessory: with soft falls and low amplitude it produces something closer to a firm caress. The wrist decides, not the object.

That is precisely why it is the second accessory and not the first. A flogger covers a far wider range of sensation than a paddle, but it requires a trained wrist. The classic failure is called **wrapping**: thrown too hard or at too much of an angle, the bundle curls around the target and the tips land on the hip or the flank, where the skin is thin and the pain has nothing to do with what was intended.

## 3. Crop and cane, precision at the cost of margin {#crop-cane}

A riding crop concentrates the impact on a tongue of a few square centimetres. The sound is dry, the sensation stinging, and above all the area struck is entirely predictable. This is the precision tool, the one that allows a single zone to be warmed progressively and returned to.

The bamboo cane pushes that logic to its limit: one rod, one contact line, a mark that appears within seconds and can stay visible for days. At ten euros it is the cheapest item in the whole selection and by far the most demanding. A cane has no place in a first kit.

Six crops and canes are listed at **1969**, from 11.40 to 54.34 euros, with round wood, braided leather or fringed handles. The selection criterion is rod stiffness: the more flexible the rod, the higher the tip speed, and the more it stings for the same effort.

## 4. The Wartenberg wheel, the odd one in {#wartenberg}

The Wartenberg wheel is not an impact accessory. It is a spiked wheel borrowed from neurological testing equipment, which rolls across the skin and produces continuous pricking without ever striking.

Its place in an impact ranking comes from how it is actually used: as a counterpoint. A session built entirely on impact saturates the struck area quickly, and sensation dulls. Alternating impact and pricking resets perception without raising intensity. It is the accessory that makes a session last without hitting harder.

Eight models are available at **1969**, from 12.80 to 26.40 euros, from the single steel wheel to a seven-wheel version. The one point of care is hygiene: the spikes touch skin, so a wheel is disinfected after every use, like any spiked accessory.

## 5. The single-tail whip, for experienced hands only {#whip}

A single-tail whip is one long lash. It is not controlled from the wrist but from the whole arm, and its tip reaches speeds that have no equivalent in the other families. A trajectory error cannot be corrected mid-stroke.

Only two models appear in the **1969** selection, at 45.70 and 47.28 euros, which reflects the real place of this accessory fairly well: a niche practice that assumes space, dry practice without a partner, and a partner who already knows their limits.

## Paddle or flogger: settling a first purchase {#paddle-or-flogger}

This is the question that comes up most often, and most guides answer "it depends". It does have an answer.

**The paddle first.** Three concrete reasons. Margin for error, already covered: a wide surface dilutes an approximate trajectory. Readability next, because a thuddy impact across a large area is easy to interpret for the person receiving it, which makes negotiating intensity far simpler in early sessions. And no wrapping: a paddle curls around nothing, what it touches is what was aimed at.

**The flogger next.** Once the movement is acquired and the safe zones internalised, it offers a range of sensation a paddle will never reach, from falls brushing the skin to a full stroke. It is the accessory a lasting practice is built on.

Both together represent 22.80 euros at entry level, which makes the trade-off fairly theoretical in practice: most first kits contain both, and the order of learning matters more than the order of purchase. ## Safety: what is not negotiable {#safety}

Zone mapping is the first rule, and it allows no exception. Safe zones are the buttocks, fleshy and muscled, the back and outer thighs, and the upper back between the shoulder blades for experienced practitioners. Forbidden zones are the kidneys and lower back, where no bone structure shields the organs, the spine, the neck, the head, the abdomen and the joints.

Progression is the second rule. An impact session always opens with low-intensity warm-up, which raises blood flow in the area and makes the strokes that follow bearable. Hitting hard from the outset on cold skin produces sharp pain without the build-up of sensation being sought.

A safe word is agreed before starting, together with a non-verbal backup signal for situations where speech is prevented. This is the RACK framework, the safety reference used across BDSM communities, which holds that risk is knowingly accepted by both parties.

Aftercare, finally, is part of the session and not of what follows it. Cooling the reddened areas, moisturising, water, and a spell of talking. The emotional drop that follows an intense session, known as subdrop, is prevented rather than repaired.
