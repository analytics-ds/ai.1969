A forty-euro board game, pulled out on a weeknight, demands a free hour, two people in the mood, and rules read aloud. A die on the bedside table demands nothing. ## 1. Dice, the family that asks for nothing {#dice}

A naughty dice game runs on a single mechanic: two dice, one carrying an action, the other a body part, and chance settles it. No rules to learn, no turn order, no winner.

That absence of structure is what makes it the best entry point. Dice create no obligation: roll once, laugh, stop there if the mood is not right. No other family forgives a badly chosen moment as well.

At 1969 the family comes in two levels of explicitness. Love to Love's **Play & Love naughty dice** opens the selection at **6.99 €**, on gentle prompts that work early in an evening. The same brand's **Let's Dice** moves up to **10.99 €** with a more direct register. Bijoux Indiscrets' **Lucky Love erotic dice**, at **7.95 €**, sit apart: the graphics are careful enough that the object can stay on a coffee table without announcing itself.

**The classic misreading** is to treat dice as a beginner's format for shy couples. The opposite is true: they work better the longer a couple has been together, because they remove the burden of asking. Nobody proposes anything, the die decides.

## 2. The dare chequebook, the only one that plays on time {#chequebook}

A dare chequebook is a pad of tear-off coupons, each carrying a proposal. One is handed over, and the other person keeps it and picks the moment.

Its mechanic is the only one in the selection that **works outside the moment**. A coupon given on Monday produces anticipation until Friday. No die does that.

1969 carries the **dare chequebooks illustrated by Apollonia Saintclair** in three registers, all at **7.99 €**: Romantic for the gentlest prompts, Kamasutra built around positions, and Kinky for the most explicit end. Choosing the register matters more here than in any other family, since the coupon will be read days after it was handed over.

**The trap worth knowing**: a chequebook is not spent in one go. Handing over five coupons on the same evening kills the anticipation that makes the format work.

## 3. Scratch cards, the compromise between chance and choice {#scratch}

Scratch cards put a couple in a position neither dice nor chequebooks create: **you choose a card without knowing what it holds, then discover it together**. The scratching itself is part of the game, slowing things down and creating a shared second of waiting.

Love to Love's **Glimmers scratch cards for couples** open the family at **14.99 €**, on a set of independent prompts. The same brand's **Chasing Memories**, at **19.99 €**, changes the logic: the cards form a progression, each building on the last. The second is not a pricier version of the first, it is a different commitment, across several evenings instead of one.

**A practical point rarely made**: a scratched card does not go back in the box. These games are consumed, unlike dice. The cost per evening is therefore higher than it looks.

## 4. The adult board game, the most demanding {#board}

Love to Love's **Spin Me 'Round**, at **39.99 €**, is the most complete format in the selection and the hardest to bring out. It needs a board laid down, rules read, and an evening nobody interrupts.

When those three conditions are met, it delivers what no other family delivers: duration, build-up, and a frame that allows proposals neither partner would have voiced alone. When they are not met, it stays in its box.

**The advice that avoids disappointment**: do not make it a first purchase. An adult board game works far better once a couple has played something else together and knows where its limits are.

## 5. The wearable toy, a family of its own {#wearable}

Love to Love's **Secret Panty 2 vibrating thong**, shown at **47.99 €** down from 59.99 €, is not a game in the sense of the others. It is a worn accessory, remote controlled, whose whole appeal rests on complicity in public. The mechanic is no longer chance or dares, it is a secret shared through an ordinary outing.

It appears in this selection because engines and couples file it in the same place, but it answers a different desire. Confusing it with a board game leads to a disappointed purchase.
