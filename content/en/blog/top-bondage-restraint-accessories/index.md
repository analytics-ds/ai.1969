## 2. Bed straps, invisible restraint {#bed-straps}

**Overall rating: ★★★★ (4.5/5)**

The principle is to run straps under the mattress and attach cuffs at the four corners. The advantage is twofold: a solid anchor point without drilling a wall, and everything dismantles in seconds.

It is the most discreet option of the list, the one that fits in a drawer and looks like nothing to a visitor. The complete kit sits around 19 euros in the **1969** range, cuffs included. Its limit is structural: straps depend on the mattress, so a very soft bed or a low base reduces available tension.

To check before buying: strap length relative to bed width, and the attachment type, snap hook rather than velcro for holding power.

## 3. The spreader bar, the underrated one {#spreader}

**Overall rating: ★★★★ (4.3/5)**

A spreader bar does not block movement, it imposes a position. Ankles or wrists stay apart at a fixed distance, constraining posture while leaving real mobility. For many couples that trade-off is more interesting than full immobilisation.

Two criteria matter. Length adjustment first, since a fixed bar suits one body and not another. Cuff detachability second, because a bar whose cuffs come off also serves separately. The adjustable steel model with detachable leather cuffs in the **1969** range sits around 57 euros and covers both.

The point of caution is balance: someone with spread ankles and bound hands cannot catch themselves. This configuration belongs on the floor or on a bed, never standing without support.

## 5. The hogtie kit, deliberate restraint {#hogtie}

**Overall rating: ★★★☆ (3.7/5)**

The accessory links a collar to the wrists, holding the hands at face height. The restraint is real but the posture stays sustainable, and the symbolic dimension counts as much as the immobilisation.

## 7. Metal handcuffs, for experienced users only {#metal-cuffs}

**Overall rating: ★★★ (3.2/5)**

They are the cheapest, between 10 and 20 euros, and the most constraining. Steel forgives nothing: no lining, pressure concentrated on a narrow band, and a real risk of marking or restricting circulation if the ratchet is too tight.

Two precautions make them acceptable: a double-locking model, which prevents the ratchet tightening accidentally, and a spare key stored somewhere both people know. Without those two, the accessory creates more problems than it solves.

## How to choose by practice {#choosing}

## Safety: what is not negotiable {#safety}

Three rules apply to every family, without exception.

Nothing around the neck that can come under tension. A collar may be worn, it must never serve as an anchor point.

A means of immediate release, at all times: a key for metal, safety scissors for rope, an accessible snap hook for leather. Whoever ties must be able to untie alone, in the dark, in thirty seconds.

A restrained person is never left alone, even briefly. And a safeword agreed in advance, with a non-verbal signal if the mouth is occupied, precedes any session.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What are the bondage restraint accessories?

Seven families cover most of the market: padded leather cuffs, bed straps with cuffs, the adjustable spreader bar, shibari rope, the hogtie kit, the neck-to-wrist restraint and metal handcuffs. ### Which restraint accessory should a beginner choose?

Padded leather cuffs or a bed strap kit. Both adjust easily, spread pressure across a wide surface and come off in seconds, which is the real criterion for a first time. The budget stays under thirty euros, and the gear remains useful as the practice evolves. Metal handcuffs and hogties are best left aside at the start.

### How do you restrain someone safely?

Four rules. Never place anything under tension around the neck. Always keep the means of release at hand, a key for metal or safety scissors for rope. Never leave a restrained person alone, not even for a minute. Agree a safeword before starting, with a non-verbal backup signal. Add circulation checks: cold, numb or pale fingers mean loosening immediately.

### Leather or metal cuffs, what is the difference?

Padded leather spreads pressure across three to four centimetres, allowing long sessions without marking the skin, and adjusts finely. Metal concentrates pressure on a narrow band, constrains more and marks faster, but delivers a strict restraint sensation some people seek. For metal, double locking and an accessible spare key are essential.
